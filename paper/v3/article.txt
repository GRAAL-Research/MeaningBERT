Unrelated Is Not Opposed:
A Signed Scale for Meaning Preservation
Anonymous Submission
Abstract
Metrics for meaning preservation report how much
meaning two sentences share, on a scale from 0 to
100. That scale cannot express opposition. Because
contradicting sentences are about the same thing, they
overlap heavily, and a released model scores “I drink
milk” against “I do not drink milk” at 58 out of 100.
We propose a signed scale on [−100, 100], obtained by
multiplying a magnitude head by 1 − α pcontra from
a three-way polarity head. The form is a product so
that unrelated sentences score 0 whatever their polarity.
Across 140 runs we find that a polarity head reaches
0.911 macro-F1, that derived-polarity augmentation
leaves task performance unchanged while raising gen-
erated sanity suites from 0.79–0.90 to 0.98–0.99 on
all seven encoders, and that composition puts 84% of
contradictions below zero where the magnitude alone
puts none.
1 Introduction
Assessing whether a rewritten sentence preserves the
meaning of its source is a prerequisite for evaluat-
ing text simplification, summarisation and translation
(Saggion, 2017). The metrics built for it, whether
embedding-based (Zhang et al., 2020; Reimers and
Gurevych, 2019), question-based (Scialom et al., 2021)
or trained end to end (Maddela et al., 2023; Beau-
chemin et al., 2023), all return a single non-negative
number. A score of 0 means the sentences have nothing
in common and a score of 100 means the meaning is
intact.
That output shape has a consequence that is easy to
state and hard to see in aggregate correlations. The
scale has no room for opposition. Two sentences that
contradict each other are, by construction, about the
same thing, so they share most of their lexical ma-
terial and most of their subject matter; a magnitude
metric reads that overlap and reports a middling-to-
high score. Measured rather than supposed: a released
model scores “I drink milk” against “I do not drink
milk” at 58.3, and the SICK contradiction “two dogs
are wrestling and hugging” against “there is no dog
wrestling and hugging” at 51.7. Both sit on the positive
half of a scale on which the positive half is the half that
means the meaning survived. Baral (2026) quantified
the effect on legal text: across minimal perturbations
that reverse legal force while preserving most tokens,
embedding metrics and BERTScore move over a small
fraction of their range, while bidirectional inference
models move substantially. The metrics are not merely
imprecise on these pairs; they are confidently wrong in
the direction that matters.
We begin by measuring the failure on the metric we
build on. The published MeaningBERT model, evalu-
ated on a held-out set of 12 000 class-balanced pairs,
ranks agreement above contradiction with an AUC
of 0.608, and on SICK (Marelli et al., 2014) alone
it reaches 0.480 with an amplitude that is negative:
it scores contradictions marginally higher than agree-
ments. A metric in production today tells its users that
a negated sentence preserves the meaning of its source.
This paper proposes to make the scale signed,
[−100, 100], where −100 denotes opposed meaning
and 0 denotes no relation. Our contributions are:
1. A signed scale obtained by composition, not by
direct supervision (Section 3). No corpus car-
ries human-annotated signed scores, so fitting one
would train a model on our own conversion rule.
We instead compose two heads whose targets both
have abundant human labels, and calibrate the
composition on the single corpus that annotates
both dimensions over the same pairs.
2. A measured diagnosis and a 140-run grid (Sec-
tion 5, Section 6). Seven encoders, ten seeds,
two corpus conditions, with class- and corpus-
stratified evaluation and source-sentence leakage
removed in both directions.
3. Three results that hold across every encoder
(Section 6): a polarity head is learnable at 0.911
macro-F1; derived-polarity augmentation buys
sanity, not performance; and inference pretrain-
ing buys reasoning under negation, not aggregate
accuracy.

2 Related Work
Meaning preservation metrics. BERTScore (Zhang
et al., 2020) computes token-level similarity from con-
textual embeddings; SARI (Xu et al., 2016) scores edit
operations; QuestEval (Scialom et al., 2021) asks ques-
tions of both texts; LENS (Maddela et al., 2023) and
MeaningBERT (Beauchemin et al., 2023) are trained
against human judgements. All of them emit a magni-
tude, and all of them are evaluated by correlation with
human ratings plus, in the case of MeaningBERT, two
sanity checks: identical sentences must score 100, un-
related sentences must score 0. Baral (2026) observes
that both checks are satisfied by any monotone function
of lexical overlap, which is precisely why they do not
catch the failure we study.
Inference and its probes. Natural language infer-
ence supplies the missing dimension. Large-scale cor-
pora (Williams et al., 2018; Nie et al., 2020) and fact
verification (Thorne et al., 2018; Schuster et al., 2021)
provide the three-way entailment, neutral and contra-
diction labels. Targeted suites test what the aggregate
hides: MoNLI (Geiger et al., 2020) probes downward
monotonicity, where a lexical substitution inside the
scope of a negation reverses the direction of entailment;
NaN-NLI (Truong et al., 2022) probes negation that
scopes below the clause. PAWS (Zhang et al., 2019)
supplies high-overlap non-paraphrases, though we ex-
clude it (Section 4).
Why neither alone. An inference model answers
whether the second sentence follows from or denies
the first, and says nothing about how much meaning
they share; a preservation metric answers the converse.
SICK (Marelli et al., 2014) is, to our knowledge, the
only resource that annotates both over the same pairs,
which makes it the only place the composition can be
calibrated.
3 A Signed Scale
What the number means. We define the score so
that +100 is identical meaning, 0 is no relation, and
−100 is opposed meaning. The decisive property is
that the negative half measures active contradiction, not
the absence of shared meaning. Two sentences about
different topics score 0, not −100: to contradict each
other, two sentences must first be about the same thing.
It follows that the score carries two pieces of infor-
mation. The magnitude asks what the sentences talk
about together, and is exactly the quantity existing met-
rics estimate. The polarity asks, once the subject is
shared, whether they assert the same thing or its nega-
tion.
Alternatives. Two other designs were considered.
Typed penalties in the style of MQM, subtracting a
cost per error category from a perfect score, give an
interpretable and natively negative output, but they re-
quire an error taxonomy annotated over the same pairs
and they change the target rather than extend it. Learn-
ing the signed scale end to end is discussed next and
rejected for a reason that applies to any such target.
Why composition rather than direct supervision.
No corpus carries signed human annotation: nobody
has asked annotators to rate “how strongly these sen-
tences say the opposite” on a continuous scale. Learn-
ing [−100, 100] end to end would require manufactur-
ing the target from categorical labels, that is, training
the model on our own conversion rule and then report-
ing the rule rather than a human judgement. Composi-
tion avoids this because both components have abun-
dant human labels of their own, and because each head
stays separately falsifiable: when the composed score
is wrong, we can say which half moved.
The form. We take
s = clip[−100,100](m × (1 − α pcontra)) , (1)
with magnitude m ∈ [0, 100] and pcontra ∈ [0, 1] from
the polarity head. A product, not a difference: an
unrelated pair has m ≈ 0 and therefore scores near
zero whatever its polarity, whereas a subtraction would
let it drift negative and destroy the distinction the scale
rests on. Table 1 works three cases.
Why α = 2. The slope governs how much contradic-
tion is needed to flip the sign. A certain contradiction
on a perfect magnitude lands at 100 × (1 − α), so
the negative half of the scale is reachable only from
α = 2 upwards; below it the scale is truncated. We
treat reachability as a constraint on the search rather
than as a term in the objective, because it is the defini-
tion of the scale and not a quantity to be traded against
the others. Section 7 reports what that constraint costs.

We considered and rejected PAWS. Its binary para-
phrase label is not a polarity judgement: two sentences
that are not paraphrases need not contradict each other,
and mapping not a paraphrase onto contradiction would
teach the head that difference amounts to opposition,
which is the failure the work exists to remove.
Derived-polarity augmentation. Our second corpus
condition adds generated pairs. Every added row has its
polarity derived, never copied, which matters because
polarity is not symmetric the way magnitude is. If A
contradicts B then B contradicts A, so a mirrored con-
tradiction remains one; but “a dog is running” entails
“an animal is running” and the converse is false, so a
mirrored entailment loses its label rather than carrying
a wrong one. We add three families in equal quotas of
25 000: mirrored contradictions, identical pairs labelled
entailment, and low-overlap random pairs labelled neu-
tral. The last is the one the signed scale depends on,
since it is what teaches that unrelated is not opposed.
Equal quotas are necessary: mirroring alone would take
the contradiction class from 50 641 to 101 282 against
51 274 entailments and teach the head to over-predict
the one class whose sign inverts the score.
Splits. Development and test splits are drawn strat-
ified by class and by corpus, 4 000 per class. Stratifi-
cation by corpus is not cosmetic: SICK is 8% of the
naturally merged test split and 0.8% of the development
split because VitaminC is fifty times its size, and se-
lecting a model on a split that is 99% VitaminC selects
for the wrong thing. Water-filling takes a small corpus
whole rather than sampling it down, raising SICK to
34% of the test split. We then remove source-sentence
leakage in both directions: VitaminC reuses the same
evidence across many claims and splits by case rather
than by evidence, so 1 662 source sentences, 5.6% of
the test groups, were also in training. We drop 2 379
training rows and 229 development rows, and verify
that train/dev, train/test and dev/test share no pair and
no source sentence, before and after augmentation.
5 Experimental Setup
We train a three-way classification head on seven en-
coders × ten seeds (42–51) × two corpus conditions,
for 140 runs. The conditions are RAW (152 060 training
rows) and AUG (227 060). Encoders span three families
and two sizes, and include both plain and inference-
pretrained variants of the same architecture so the effect
of inference pretraining is measured at constant size.
Checkpoints already fine-tuned for three-way infer-
ence keep their head when its class order matches ours,
which is free and on-task supervision; where the pub-
lished order is reversed we permute the classifier rows
rather than silently training from a permuted head. All
runs use two epochs, learning rate 10−5, effective batch
32 and a maximum length of 256 tokens, on fp32 Pascal
hardware.
Compute. The grid is 140 training runs, roughly 500
GPU-hours in fp32 on two Pascal cards (GTX 1080
Ti, Quadro P5000) and one Titan Xp. A base-encoder
run takes 68 minutes on the RAW condition and 109
on AUG; a large-encoder run takes 318 and 489. The
composition in Section 7 trains nothing and fits one
parameter: it is two forward passes over 4 116 pairs
and runs on CPU in under half an hour. We report

this because the asymmetry is the practical argument

We report macro-F1 on the stratified test split, ac-
curacy on three generated sanity suites built from test-
split sentences (identical, unrelated, mirrored), and
accuracy on the two held-out probes. Significance uses
Welch’s t-test (Welch, 1947) between conditions and a
paired test by seed between architectures, with Cohen’s
d beside every p-value: with ten seeds and standard
deviations near 0.002, a difference of 0.001 reaches
significance while meaning nothing.
6 Results
The polarity head is learnable. The best config-
uration reaches 0.911 ± 0.001 macro-F1 on a class-
balanced test set of 12 000 pairs (Table 3). The seed
standard deviation of 0.0014 over ten runs indicates
the result is a property of the configuration and not of
a draw.
Augmentation buys sanity, not performance. On
the four DeBERTa encoders the macro-F1 difference
between conditions lies between −0.002 and +0.001
and is not significant (p from 0.17 to 0.74); on the other
three it is significant but under one hundredth. The san-
ity suites move in a different regime entirely (Table 4,
Figure 1): +0.084 to +0.200, p < 10−4 on all seven
encoders, with d from 9.9 to 81.1. The effect sizes are
what settle the matter. On RoBERTa-baseSTS-B, d = 81
places the shift two orders of magnitude above the seed-
to-seed noise, whereas the largest task effect anywhere
in the grid is d = 2.2 for a difference of 0.007. Every
encoder converges to 0.983–0.988 regardless of where
it started, and the clearest cases are the weakest models:
RoBERTa-baseSTS-B moves from 0.787 to 0.987 and
BERT-base from 0.794 to 0.983.
This is the behaviour the augmentation was built
for. It does not make the head better at the task; it
repairs how the head behaves at the ends of the scale,
which is where a signed metric is read and where the
unrelated-as-opposed failure lives.
Which suite moves. The aggregate hides where the
gain comes from, so Table 5 separates the three fami-
lies. Identical pairs are essentially solved before aug-
mentation, which is the result one should expect from
a trivial case. The gain is concentrated on the other
two. Unrelated pairs rise from 0.92–0.96 to 0.997: be-
fore augmentation, between four and eight percent of
sentence pairs with nothing in common are read as con-
tradictory, which on the signed scale means returning
a negative score where the truth is zero. Mirrored con-
tradictions rise from 0.74 to 0.96, and on BERT-base
from 0.50, meaning that the symmetry of contradiction
is not something these encoders hold on their own.
Inference pretraining buys reasoning under nega-
tion. Paired by seed, DeBERTa-v3-largeNLI beats its
plain counterpart by +0.002 macro-F1 (p = 0.002,
d = 1.39) and by +0.121 on MoNLI (p < 10−4,
d = 2.58). The aggregate metric barely registers an
advantage that the monotonicity probe shows plainly.
At base size the pattern inverts: the task gain is larger
(+0.007, p = 0.005) and the MoNLI difference is not
significant. We read this as inference pretraining sup-
plying a capability that the aggregate test set does not
exercise, which is the argument for reporting probes
alongside it.
How the models fail on monotonicity. MoNLI accu-
racy is low for every configuration, and the confusion
matrix shows the failure is not the one we expected.
The heads almost never call these pairs contradictions:
on DeBERTa-v3-largeNLI, 0.2% of true entailments are
predicted contradiction. They have not learned that the
token “not” signals opposition. What they fail is the
reversal itself: 93% of true entailments are predicted
neutral, because under negation the more specific term
follows from the more general one rather than the other
way round, and the heads apply the unreversed rule.
This is a clean negative result, and it is what the probe
exists to expose: no aggregate number on our test split
distinguishes a head that has learned the relation from
one that has learned its unnegated half.
Diagnosis of deployed metrics. Table 6 scores three
released magnitude models on both tasks at once: Pear-

the ability to rank agreement above contradiction on
our test split. The two columns are close to indepen-

BERT-base variant, which is a competent magnitude

SICK alone the published model reaches 0.480 with

no contradiction at all below the line, which is not a
weakness of that particular model but a property of a
non-negative scale. Composition puts 84.4% of them
where they belong while retaining r = 0.760 with
human relatedness on the unflipped pairs. The cost
of requiring the full scale is visible and small: α =
1.25 scores 2.3% higher on the combined objective and
truncates the negative half at −25.
8 Limitations
Augmentation costs monotonicity. The gain in san-
ity is paid for on MoNLI, which drops on six of
seven encoders, by −0.068 on DeBERTa-v3-large and
−0.037 on its inference-pretrained variant. Generated
identical and unrelated pairs teach the easy ends of the
scale, not the reversal of entailment under negation. We
report the trade rather than resolve it.
No signed gold standard. The composition is cali-
brated against three necessary conditions, not against
human signed judgements, because none exist. A cor-
pus of human-rated signed pairs would let the form of
Equation 1 be tested rather than argued, and we regard
collecting one as the natural next step.
The bridge is thin on one side. SICK supplies only
495 development rows, of which 229 shared source
sentences with its own test portion and were removed,
leaving the development split at 2.3% SICK against
34% in test. Model selection is therefore driven by
VitaminC while the test set is not. This is a property of
the available data, not a design choice, and it is the first
thing to watch should the two numbers diverge.
Scope. All experiments are English, sentence-level,
and run in fp32 on Pascal GPUs; we did not evaluate
encoders requiring more recent hardware. Two of the
seven encoders wedge one of our cards reproducibly,
which we worked around by hardware gating rather
than diagnosed.
9 Conclusion
A meaning-preservation metric that cannot distinguish
a paraphrase from a negation is not imprecise, it is
confidently wrong on the pairs where precision matters,
and we show that the metric deployed today has exactly
this property. Making the scale signed does not require
a better magnitude model; it requires a second head and
an explicit rule for combining them. The combination
is cheap, since it trains nothing beyond a three-way
classifier and fits one parameter, and it moves 84% of
contradictions to the correct side of zero where the
magnitude alone moves none. What it does not yet
have is a human-annotated signed target against which
the combining rule itself could be tested.
